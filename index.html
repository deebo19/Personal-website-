Quality, start to finish

I'm a big believer in shift-left: bugs found earlier in the SDLC are cheaper to fix. Here's how I own quality at every stage.

  1. 01

    Discovery & requirements

    Involved from the discovery phase: design reviews, requirements analysis and writing acceptance criteria before code is written.

    Output: testable acceptance criteria, early risk flags

  2. 02

    Test strategy & planning

    Test strategies and plans that decide what's automated vs. manual, increasingly generated and maintained with AI tooling.

    Output: test strategy, test plan, budget priorities

  3. 03

    Test design

    Test cases in TestRail and Zephyr kept current with every feature, with candidates for automation identified.

    Output: test cases, automation backlog

  4. 04

    Environments & devices

    Every platform users touch: iOS, Android, web, Apple TV, smart TVs, set-top boxes, consoles, Apple Watch and VR. That includes VPN setups to test localisation across countries.

    Output: device matrix, env & build access

  5. 05

    Execution & automation

    Across the testing pyramid: unit, integration, API, exploratory, performance and UX. Automation runs in CI/CD, now including agentic tests on daily schedules.

    Output: automated suites, CI runs, test reports

  6. 06

    Defect triage

    Bugs triaged and prioritised with the team, with P0/P1 issues escalated to management quickly and clearly.

    Output: prioritised bug backlog

  7. 07

    Regression & release

    Regression cycles run across devices and OS versions, plus release management through Staging, UAT and Production, delivered to hard deadlines.

    Output: release sign-off, on-time launches

  8. 08

    Metrics & improvement

    QA OKRs and metrics driven with stakeholders, QA Excellence initiatives across the org, and results communicated clearly to management.

    Output: OKRs, quality metrics, process change
